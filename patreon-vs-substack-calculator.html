Patreon vs Substack: Platform Economics for Creators

Both Patreon and Substack have empowered independent writers, podcasters, educators, and artists to build direct recurring subscriber revenue. However, their monetization fee structures and payment processor mechanics differ considerably.

Patreon Fee Tiers

Patreon charges an 8% platform cut (Pro) or 12% cut (Premium), in addition to standard payment processing fees (2.9% + $0.30 or micro-payment rates).

Substack Platform Cut

Substack charges a flat 10% fee on recurring paid subscriptions, with processing routed directly to your connected Stripe account at 2.9% + $0.30.

Understanding the Fixed $0.30 Charge on Low Tier Pledges

If you offer low-priced $3 or $5 monthly tiers, fixed credit card fees ($0.30) consume a massive percentage of your pledge (6% to 10% on payment fees alone!). Encouraging annual billing or higher price points ($8–$15/month) drastically increases your net creator take-home pay.

Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage does Substack take from paid newsletters? ▼
Substack charges a flat 10% platform fee on all paid subscriptions. Credit card processing fees (2.9% + $0.30 via Stripe) are separate.
Is Substack free to use for free email newsletters? ▼
Yes. Substack is 100% free with no email sending or subscriber caps if you do not turn on paid subscriptions.
Which platform is better for community vs written content? ▼
Patreon is generally better suited for multimedia creators offering community Discord roles and exclusive video/audio tiers, while Substack excels for written essays and email-first newsletters.
Do I own my subscriber email list on both platforms? ▼
Substack allows you to export your full email subscriber list at any time with zero friction. Patreon allows member data exports, but email communication is typically designed through their internal messaging feed.
